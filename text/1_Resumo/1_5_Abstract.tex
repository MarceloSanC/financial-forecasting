\begin{resumo}[Abstract]

This work characterizes the probabilistic calibration of a Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) applied to multi-horizon daily return forecasting, compares it with a hierarchy of reference models, and describes the relative contribution of indicator families. The research problem stems from the fact that, for daily returns of liquid stocks, the predictable component of the mean is minimal, so evaluations restricted to point metrics tend to measure noise; the scientific object is therefore the predictive distribution rather than point accuracy. The scope is single-asset (one model per asset), with AAPL as the pilot asset and horizons $h+1$ and $h+7$. The methodology integrates market data, technical indicators, news sentiment estimated with FinBERT, and accounting fundamentals, with temporal causality verified feature by feature, and trains the TFT in quantile mode over a dense grid of seven levels, with monotone rearrangement of the quantiles. The experimental protocol uses expanding-window walk-forward validation with purging and embargo in trading days and a dedicated calibration set, six folds and ten seeds, strict alignment by target timestamp, and comparison against a pre-declared hierarchy of baselines: zero return, historical mean, AR(1), EWMA volatility, historical empirical quantiles, and quantile gradient boosting. Calibration is judged, per horizon, by a Wilson-band gate on both tails of the central 80\% interval, always reported with its declared power against pre-registered minimum deviations. Pinball loss is the primary metric and CRPS is complementary; inference relies on the Diebold-Mariano test (HAC/HLN, one-sided) with Holm adjustment and the Model Confidence Set, and native calibration is contrasted with conformalized quantile regression as an empirical-coverage benchmark. Feature-family contribution is read from the agreement between permutation importance and ablation with retraining, with Variable Selection Network weights as a complementary description. The verdict is issued by a scorecard applied mechanically under a pre-registration frozen, hashed, and anchored before the tables judged by the verdict are generated, and refutation of the hypotheses is treated as a valid result. In the confirmatory round, calibration of the central 80\% interval was not rejected at either horizon, with power above 0.97 against 5-percentage-point coverage deviations; violations, however, cluster in time, so calibration is marginal rather than conditional. The TFT beat the naive baselines at both horizons but not the strong comparators: at $h+1$ it was excluded from the Model Confidence Set, and at $h+7$ it tied with the strong comparators and was declared primary winner under the pre-registered rule, although the EWMA-volatility baseline had the lowest mean loss at both horizons. Feature-family contribution did not vary detectably across horizons, and the technical family, which contains the volatility measures, was the most important under both methods. The results indicate that the model's value lies in a calibrated description of the scale of the distribution, not in skill gains over simple volatility models.
