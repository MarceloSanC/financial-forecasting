\begin{resumo}[Abstract]

Forecasting the daily return of a stock is notoriously hard: the predictable part of the mean is minimal, and calling the direction of the price barely beats chance. What can be forecast more reliably is uncertainty, that is, the interval within which the return should fall. This work evaluates whether a deep learning model, the Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT), produces calibrated probabilistic forecasts of the daily returns of Apple (AAPL), one and seven trading days ahead, and whether they beat simpler reference models. The TFT receives market data, technical indicators, news sentiment scored by FinBERT and accounting fundamentals, and forecasts seven quantiles of the return distribution. The evaluation uses temporal validation with six test periods between 2019 and 2025, ten independent trainings of the model and six comparators, ranging from naive forecasts to a volatility model (EWMA) and a quantile gradient boosting model. The decision rules (calibration, paired statistical comparison and contribution of the data families) were registered and frozen before the confirmatory round, and the verdict tables were reproduced bit for bit. Calibration of the 80\% interval was not rejected at either horizon: observed coverage was 0.818, and the test had a probability above 0.97 of detecting deviations of 5 percentage points; violations, however, cluster in time. The TFT clearly beat the naive forecasts, but not the strong comparators: the EWMA model, which describes only recent volatility, had the lowest loss at both horizons, and the TFT tied statistically with the best models only at the seven-day horizon. An exploratory analysis that combined the ten trainings did not change this picture. The family of volatility-related indicators was the most informative, and the contribution of the families did not vary detectably across horizons. We conclude that the value of a probabilistic model of daily returns lies in a calibrated description of the scale of the distribution, which a simple volatility model already captures well, and that the complexity of the TFT did not translate into predictive gains over that model in this single-asset setting.

\textbf{Keywords}: Temporal Fusion Transformer. Quantile forecasting. Probabilistic calibration. Financial returns. Preregistration.

\end{resumo}
