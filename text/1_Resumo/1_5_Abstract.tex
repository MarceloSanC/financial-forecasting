\begin{resumo}[Abstract]

Forecasting the daily return of a stock is notoriously hard: the predictable part of the mean is minimal, and calling the direction of the price barely beats chance. What can be forecast more reliably is uncertainty, that is, the interval within which the return should fall. This work evaluates whether a deep learning model, the Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT), produces calibrated probabilistic forecasts of the daily returns of Apple (AAPL), one and seven trading days ahead, and whether they beat simpler reference models. The TFT receives market data, technical indicators, news sentiment scored by FinBERT and accounting fundamentals, and forecasts seven quantiles of the return distribution. The evaluation uses temporal validation with six test periods between 2019 and 2025, ten training seeds of the model in each period and six comparators, ranging from naive forecasts to a volatility model (EWMA) and a quantile gradient boosting model. The decision rules (calibration, paired statistical comparison and contribution of the data families) were registered and frozen before any performance result was known, and the verdict tables were reproduced bit for bit in the same execution environment. Calibration of the 80\% interval was not rejected at either horizon: averaged over seeds, observed coverage was 0.818, and the criterion had a probability above 0.97 of detecting deviations of 5 percentage points (an optimistic value at the seven-trading-day horizon). Violations, however, cluster in time, and the coverage of individual seeds ranged from 0.772 to 0.872 at the one-day horizon. The TFT clearly beat the naive forecasts, but not the strong comparators: the EWMA model, which describes only recent volatility, had the lowest loss at both horizons, and the TFT tied statistically with the best models only at the seven-trading-day horizon. An exploratory analysis that combined the ten seeds did not change this picture. The technical-indicator family, which includes the volatility measures, had the largest estimated importance, and the contribution of the families did not vary detectably across horizons. The results indicate that the value of a probabilistic model of daily returns lies in describing the scale of the distribution well, with calibration not rejected, which a simple volatility model already captures well, except in the extreme tail, and that the complexity of the TFT did not translate into predictive gains over that model in this single-asset setting.

\textbf{Keywords}: Temporal Fusion Transformer; quantile forecasting; probabilistic calibration; volatility; market risk; financial returns; preregistration.

\end{resumo}
